title: "The windmills that I never got to see"
date: 2026-09-13
---

r/punebikers sub-reddit keeps on feeding me great riding routes around Pune. It's there that I discovered a riding circuit that goes through serene roads and eventually leads to a wind farm with these huge, tall wind turbines. The kid in me got excited at the thought of getting to see windmills from so close. Alas, little did I know that I was about to get slightly disappointed.

This morning, I woke up a little earlier than I usually do, freshened up, had some tea and hit the roads. Of the three routes suggested by Google Maps, I decided to take the Old Pune-Mumbai Highway towards Dehu Road Cantonment and ahead. As soon as I hit the road, I realised I wouldn't be able to ride without putting my raincoat on. It was just misty when I started, but soon it turned into a light drizzle. I put my raincoat on and resumed the journey.

<img src="/assets/images/windmill-farm/mist-and-fog.jpeg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;">

When I entered the Dehu Cantonment area, I thought to myself about the numerous such roads and places in and around Pune that I have known about but never actually visited. The roads in the Dehu Cantonment area are pretty much what you'd expect from a military establishment: slick and well maintained. But as soon as the cantonment area ends, the roads make you aware of the divide between military and civilian ways of life.

The road further led me past Siddhant Institute of Engineering and I also encountered Kendriya Vidyalaya Sudumbre along the way. The school is located within the NDRF campus. The NDRF, or National Disaster Response Force, is a specialised force that responds to natural and man-made disasters, carrying out rescue and relief operations. The 5th Battalion of the NDRF is based at Sudambare in Maval and has Maharashtra and Goa within its area of responsibility. It was only when I later read about some of the NDRF's operations that I realised just how critical their intervention has been during disasters, not just in Maharashtra but across the country.

From there, the roads took me through a part of Chakan MIDC, which stretches towards the Talegaon side, and I came across several industrial units. Opera Energy, Neuron, Atlas Copco were a few that I remember. I was a little amused because I had always thought of Chakan MIDC as being much farther to my east. But there are multiple phases to the industrial area, and the part I was riding through was Chakan Industrial Area Phase II.

<img src="/assets/images/windmill-farm/Chakan-midc-phase2.jpeg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;">

Although there are a lot of manufacturing plants around, they did little to tarnish the scenic beauty around them. Some of these plants seem to have blended beautifully into the landscape and have some breathtaking views around them. Anyway, I kept cruising, stopping every once in a while to click pictures. Throughout the ride, I was accompanied by light showers and lush green mountains.

I reached a small village named Shive, where I saw a bit of a commotion. When I got closer, a few riders were helping another fellow rider get from an auto into a car, while some of the villagers were helping them out. I stopped by to see if I could help, but there were already enough people around. Upon asking around, I learnt that a fellow rider had slipped a little ahead of Shive and injured his leg.

Yes, the rains had made the roads quite slippery. And although the showers were light, the tiny drops hitting your face continuously while riding can almost feel like little stings. I made a mental note to be fully present while riding and to slow down considerably. I hope and wish the guy who injured his leg is doing fine now.

My destination was another 30 minutes away and I couldn't wait to see the huge wind turbines. I kept riding, surrounded by mountains and clouds that had come down halfway to cover the mountain tops. The backwaters of the Bhama Askhed Dam stretched across the landscape, making for a spectacular view. It was such a pleasant sight.

<img src="/assets/images/windmill-farm/Bhama-askhed-backwaters.jpeg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;">

<img src="/assets/images/windmill-farm/my-jawa-bhama-askhed-dam.jpeg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;">

I was just three minutes away from my destination when I could hear the eerie sound of the wind turbines in an otherwise calm and serene surrounding. But I couldn't see them anywhere. The clouds and fog had made it impossible to locate the turbines on the mountain tops from below. A passerby asked me what I was looking for and, after I explained, he told me that Google Maps had got it wrong. The way to the wind farm was about 2 km behind me.

<img src="/assets/images/windmill-farm/windmills-hidden-behind-clouds.jpeg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;">

I turned back and kept asking people where the turn leading to the wind farm was. Every single person I asked told me to drop the idea because the last 3 km stretch was muddy and I would probably get stuck if I tried going further. They said it would have been manageable for off-roading had it not been raining continuously since the previous night. I stopped right before the road that takes you to the wind farm. Even at the beginning of the road, there were deep, muddy furrows. I decided to avoid it.

So close, yet so far. I was a little dismayed, but it was never about the destination, right? The journey itself had been so pleasant and beautiful. I chinned up and began my return journey.

The mild disappointment of not seeing the wind farm had made me famished, and I was looking for a good place to take a short break. But I couldn't find a spot that made me want to stop. There were a few eateries along the way, but nothing really caught my fancy. So I kept riding until I reached Chakan MIDC Phase II, where one road leads towards Talegaon Dabhade and another towards the Chakan side.

I was a bit confused about which road to take and eventually decided to take the road I had come from. A few kilometres into the ride, I stopped to check again if there was another route I could take. And this is where I kinda messed up.

I found a road that seemed to lead through the river bodies and backwaters around Jadhavwadi Dam. Little did I know that I would have to push my Jawa through a lot of off-roading, ankle-deep water and muddy, pothole-ridden roads. I kept cursing myself for falling prey to Google Maps' mirage. After about 15–20 minutes of stressful riding, I was finally back on better roads. I did a quick direction check only to realise that I was on my way to Talegaon. The very route I was trying to avoid. Lol. Made peace with it.

<img src="/assets/images/windmill-farm/jadhavwadi-dam-view-road-muddy.jpeg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;">

I chuckled when I passed a place called "Induri" and heard the locals pronounce it as "Indori". I wondered how it got its name from a place that is so far away in another state. Probably just a random thought, but these are the kind of things your mind wanders into when you're riding alone. As I mentioned earlier, I was famished, so I stopped at a Bangalore Iyengar Bakery and grabbed a quick bite. I caught my breath, stretched a bit and then cruised back home.

<img src="/assets/images/windmill-farm/bridge-over-indrayani.jpeg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;">

I was accompanied by rain for most of the ride, except for the stretch from Talegaon to Pune. All in all, I rode just short of 100 km over about four hours. I didn't get to see the wind farm.

But somewhere along the way, the ride reminded me of something I keep learning over and over again: you can't always control what the destination looks like, whether you'll reach it, or whether it will even turn out to be what you imagined.

But you can still make the journey worthwhile.
