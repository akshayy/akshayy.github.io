Of all the Spider-Man outings I have seen, the one I found the least enjoyable is Spider-Noir, a series on Amazon Prime Video.

I say "least enjoyable" because it's not like I didn't enjoy it at all. There were bits and pieces here and there that I really liked. But overall, it wasn't as enjoyable or as compelling as the other Spider-Man movies and animated shows I've seen so far.

Spider-Noir is a neo-noir superhero series based on the Marvel comic character Spider-Man Noir. Unlike most other Spider-Man outings, we don't have Peter Parker here. We have Ben Reilly, played by Nicolas Cage.

Set in 1930s New York, Spider-Noir follows Ben Reilly, a down-on-his-luck private detective who was once the city's only superhero. After a personal tragedy, Ben gives up his superhero life and settles into a relatively dull existence as a private investigator.

Ben is dragged back into action when he is hired to investigate Cat Hardy, a nightclub singer and femme fatale (a new word I learnt while writing this :P - it means a woman who uses feminine wiles such as beauty, charm and seduction to achieve what she wants) who has ties to the criminal underworld. She also happens to have a connection to Silvermane, the main villain who has a history with Ben.

Along the way, Ben falls for Cat Hardy and starts believing that perhaps he could have a future with her. But Cat has another love interest, Flint Marko, who is essentially this series' version of Sandman. Ben soon discovers that Flint Marko isn't the only person in New York with unusual abilities. Silvermane has surrounded himself with people who have their own powers and uses them as muscle to further his grip over the city. And, of course, our reluctant hero, Spider-Noir, has to step back into action.

Every episode is available in both black-and-white and color. Whichever version you choose, the show has that smoky, shadowy atmosphere of a classic noir film set in 1930s New York.

Spider-Noir is a fun watch, but only in parts. Unlike Peter Parker, Ben Reilly is not your friendly neighborhood Spider-Man. He doesn't have the playfulness, the vibrant energy or the restless nature that we have come to associate with Spider-Man in the movies. He comes across as laid-back, unwilling and vulnerable. The show doesn't rely too heavily on Spider-Man nostalgia. There are familiar characters and familiar villains, but the series tries to carve its own path. The story is engaging for most of its run, and the screenplay, along with the shadowy aesthetics of its 1930s setting, is what makes it worth watching.

The performances, however, are okay. Nothing remarkable. Just okay. Two of the performances that annoyed me the most were Nicolas Cage's and the guy's who plays the villain named "Megawatt". Cage's charisma, at least for me, is nowhere close to what we saw in National Treasure (I loved the National Treasure movies). It almost feels like someone forced him to act in this one. His dialogue delivery is heavily nasal, and honestly, it just wasn't that much fun watching Nicolas Cage here. As for Megawatt, the way the character is performed makes it feel like he is trying too hard. Imagine someone delivering almost every dialogue in the same monotone, with a shrillness in his voice, while constantly trying to sound scary and threatening. It gets annoying after a while.

The performances that act as a saving grace are Li Jun Li as Cat Hardy and Brendan Gleeson as Silvermane. Honestly, Nicolas Cage and Brendan Gleeson were the only two familiar faces I recognized in this series. I hadn't seen the work of most of the other actors before. But every time Cat Hardy comes on screen, Li Jun Li delivers. The same goes for Brendan Gleeson as Silvermane. Silvermane's performance is particularly good. It is very restrained, and Gleeson does a great job playing someone who is intimidating without constantly having to shout or threaten people. He plays someone who rules with an iron grip. Every time Silvermane and Nicolas Cage confront each other, I found myself paying more attention to Silvermane. He somehow manages to overshadow Cage in those scenes.

Overall, Spider-Noir is a good watch. If you love stories set in the early decades of the 20th century, especially stories with that classic noir setup, smoky atmosphere and shadowy characters, you will probably find Spider-Noir enjoyable. But if you are expecting something like the Spider-Man movies, Spider-Noir certainly won't give you that.
