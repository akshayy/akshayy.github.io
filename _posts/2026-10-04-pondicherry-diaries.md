<h2 id="day-1">Day 1: Pune to Chennai, and Chennai to Pondicherry</h2>

Me, my wife and our daughter - Ahilya went on a 4 day trip to Pondicherry. Our Pondicherry trip actually spanned five days, from September 30 to October 4. We had an early morning flight from Pune to Chennai. Since we had to reach the airport at least an hour before our scheduled departure, we had to wake up at the unearthly hour of 2 AM. I thought we'd simply lift Ahilya out of bed without waking her up, but she woke up at around 3 AM and didn't sleep again until we boarded the flight. It had been a while since my last early morning flight, and I realised how much I'd missed that little thrill of catching a flight at the crack of dawn. After a smooth security check and baggage drop, along with a few tantrums from Ahilya, we finally boarded the plane.

Ahilya kept nagging us for one thing or another until the plane hit the runway. I asked her where we were headed, and she kept saying, "Tombecherry!" That was enough to melt our hearts. She's growing up so fast, and the thought that one day she won't mispronounce words like this anymore genuinely breaks my heart. The celestial views above the clouds calmed her down a bit. Eventually, the exhaustion from the lack of sleep caught up with all three of us, and sleep beckoned. We caught up on some rest for an hour or so, only to wake up one by one as the plane began its descent. We freshened up at Chennai Airport and headed out to find our pre-booked cab to Puducherry. After a bit of trouble locating the cab and its driver, we finally set off.

I was hoping we'd take the East Coast Road (ECR), which runs along parts of the Bay of Bengal coastline and connects Chennai with Puducherry and other destinations further south. I'd been looking forward to the scenic drive. However, our driver suggested that some sections were under construction and bumpy, and that taking that route would add around 30 minutes to our journey. At least, that's what I could make out from what he said. We decided against taking the ECR and ended up paying around ₹220 in tolls on the route we took, although we did reach our destination earlier than we might have otherwise. Oh yes, by the way, barely 20 minutes into the journey, I requested a pit stop. We stopped at a place called Vasanta Bhavan, which has been around since 1974, for breakfast. I'm glad we did. We had some of the best vada sambar, idli, Mysore masala dosa and filter coffee at this place. Amazing taste!

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day1/VasantaBhavan.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Vasanta Bhavan, Chennai">

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day1/VasantaBhavan1.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Breakfast at Vasanta Bhavan">

Fully satiated, we resumed our journey towards Puducherry. All three of us fell asleep again in no time, although I kept waking up every now and then to check on Ahilya. Along the way, I tried striking up a conversation with our driver, but the language barrier made it difficult to have a continuous, free-flowing conversation. We did manage to talk a little about Thalapathy Vijay, the DMK and politics.

We reached our hotel, Le Royal Park in Puducherry, in about three hours, including the 30-minute breakfast stop.

Fortunately, we got an early check-in and settled into our room. We decided to have lunch at the hotel itself, which turned out to be a bad idea. After resting for a bit, we booked an auto to take us to White Town. For a distance of just 3.1 km, the auto driver charged us ₹200. Crazy! We headed straight to INTACH, an organisation that works towards the conservation and restoration of heritage buildings and other historic structures in India. We enquired about getting a guide to help us explore White Town. They gave us the number of a guide, and we decided to meet him ten minutes later at the beachfront, which was just 500 metres away.

Our guide, Mr Ashok Panda, was an elderly gentleman in his sixties. His greying hair and years of experience showed in the way he spoke about Puducherry. He knew a great deal about the town's history and took us on a walking tour of the beautiful streets of White Town, including Rue Dumas, Rue Surcouf, Rue Romain Rolland and Bussy Street, explaining quite a few interesting things along the way. 'Rue' is French for 'street', and many of these streets are named after significant people from the town's colonial history. These streets have a distinct, quiet charm and an aesthetic appeal of their own. There are quite a few colonial-era villas and homes with colourful exteriors, and the neighbourhood has a very different feel from the rest of the town.

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day1/LePondicherry.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Le Pondichery, a boutique on the streets of White Town">

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day1/WhiteTown1.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="White Town, Pondicherry">

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day1/Whitetown2.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Colonial-era villas, White Town">

Our guide shared several interesting observations:
- Not all the colourful villas and houses we see here are from the colonial era. Some are newer constructions.
- New constructions and restorations in heritage areas have to follow applicable heritage rules and guidelines to preserve the architectural character of the neighbourhood.
- We noticed a Starbucks on Bussy Street, and it looked nothing like the typical Starbucks outlet. There were no glass façades, and its exterior blended in well with the colourful surroundings. It was interesting to see a global brand adapt itself to the character of the neighbourhood.
- Heritage buildings cannot simply be demolished or altered at will even if you own them. The heritage regulations and permissions govern what can be done to them.

We also visited the Church of Our Lady of Angels (Notre-Dame-des-Anges), a beautiful church dating back to the 1850s. The church opens onto a cemetery, with a statue of Joan of Arc standing in front and the beach just beyond it. The setting was quite striking.

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day1/Church.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Church of Our Lady of Angels">

Some of the other places we visited were the French War Memorial, the old Town Hall area and a government girls' school established during the French period, which has a view of the beach.

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day1/FrenchWarMemorial.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="French War Memorial">

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day1/Hotel De Ville.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Hotel De Ville, the old Town Hall">

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day1/WhiteTown4.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Government girls' school from the French period">

We ended our walking tour near the French War Memorial, overlooking the Promenade. By then, Ahilya was exhausted and asking for ice cream. Luckily, we spotted a small softy shop next to Baker Street, a well-known bakery in Puducherry. We indulged in some Cinnamon roll while Ahilya enjoyed her ice cream. The Cinnamon roll wasn't overly sweet and seemed very fresh. We sat on the paved promenade running parallel to the seashore for a few minutes to relax.

This stretch of White Town, around Rock Beach and Promenade Beach, is probably the most lively part of the neighbourhood. The seafront promenade stretches for roughly 1.5 km, and vehicles are restricted along much of it. You see people walking, running, chatting and simply relaxing by the sea. I took a deep breath and tried to take it all in. You hardly get a chance to step away from the hustle and bustle of city life, and being somewhere that puts pedestrians first and keeps much of the seafront free from traffic felt incredibly peaceful.

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day1/RockBeach.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Rock Beach, Pondicherry">

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day1/PromenadeBeach.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Promenade Beach, Pondicherry">

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day1/WhiteTown5.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Evening at White Town">

After a while, we got up and walked a few hundred metres to a restaurant called Cilantro for dinner. We tried a French dish called *poulet avec sauce aux champignons*, which was essentially chicken stuffed with spinach and served with mushroom sauce. It was good and, more importantly, something different from what we usually eat.

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day1/Pouvlet.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Poulet avec sauce aux champignons at Cilantro">

We booked an Ola to get back to our hotel. We were so exhausted that we crashed into bed and fell asleep almost instantly.

<h2 id="day-2">Day 2: Pondicherry to Auroville</h2>

All three of us slept for a good ten+ hours, thanks to the exhaustion from travelling and walking around White Town the previous day. We woke up at around 7 AM and were ready by 8 AM. We headed to the hotel's restaurant for the complimentary breakfast. There was a decent spread of South Indian dishes, fruits, confectionery, and live counters where they made dosas, uttapams and various omelettes to order. I feasted on vada sambar. In fact, I lost count of how many vadas I must have had. This was, without a doubt, the best medu vada sambar I'd ever had in my life. The rest of the spread was strictly okay. I particularly missed a good cup of adrak chai. We ate whatever appealed to us, to our heart's content, and took our own sweet time doing so.

After breakfast, I stepped out to rent a scooter from a nearby vendor. I got a Suzuki Access for ₹400 a day, and the vendor even filled it up with two litres of petrol.

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day2/Suzuki-access.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="The rented Suzuki Access">

We put Auroville as our destination on Google Maps and set off at around 9:45 AM. Auroville was roughly 12 km from our hotel. We've always relied on Google Maps to get around, and this time, too, we thought reaching the place would be a breeze. Given that Auroville is such an important attraction and one of the defining identities of the region, we expected the approach to be relatively straightforward. Unfortunately, the lack of clear signboards and recognisable landmarks made the last stretch rather frustrating. We ended up going back and forth along the same road during the final couple of kilometres. Google Maps completely lost the plot and kept showing us roads that didn't exist. Thankfully, a couple who had gone through the same ordeal spotted us struggling and came to our rescue. They directed us to the right route, and within five minutes, we were at the Auroville Visitors Centre.

From the parking area, you have to walk for about 10–15 minutes to reach the place where passes are issued for the Matrimandir Viewing Point.

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day2/auroville-entrace-right-path-cafe.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Walking in to the Auroville Visitors Centre">

The Matrimandir is a striking spherical structure that stands at the centre of Auroville's planned township. Its exterior is covered with golden discs, while its inner chamber is designed for silent concentration and meditation. The structure also has a heliostat system that tracks the sun and directs sunlight into the inner chamber, where it is focused through a central arrangement of glass. The viewing point, however, gives you an exterior view of the Matrimandir from a distance.

We collected our passes and waited for the shuttle. Although the viewing point is accessible to visitors without advance booking, entering the Matrimandir's inner chamber for meditation requires prior booking, generally requested at least a week in advance. The inner chamber also has age restrictions, so Ahilya wouldn't have been able to enter anyway.

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day2/Shuttle-matrimandir.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="The shuttle to the Matrimandir viewing point">

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day2/Screenshot-2026-10-11-matrimandir-model.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="A model of the Matrimandir">

The shuttle took us to the viewing point, where we got a clear view of this astonishing structure. It was late morning, and the Matrimandir shone like a giant golden ball. I couldn't help imagining how it might look in the evening, with the changing light reflecting off its golden exterior.

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day2/matrimandir.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="The Matrimandir, Auroville">

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day2/Matrimandir1.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="The golden discs of the Matrimandir">

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day2/Matrimandir2.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="View of the Matrimandir from the viewing point">

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day2/Matrimandir3.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="The Matrimandir amid the greens of Auroville">

We spent around 20 minutes there, clicked a few pictures and returned by shuttle. There were other tours available, including guided visits covering different aspects of Auroville's residential community, community kitchen and green initiatives. We decided to skip them because they seemed a little expensive, and it was already getting late. We had lunch at a café called The Right Path, which we had spotted near the entrance to the Visitors Centre.

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day2/Right-Path-Cafe.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="The Right Path Cafe, near Auroville">

After a sumptuous meal, we rode back to our hotel and were in our room by around 4 PM.

We rested for a bit, and then Ahilya insisted that we go swimming. The pool water was lukewarm, making it perfect for a relaxing swim. Ahilya could barely contain her excitement. I had to hold her hand and ease her into the water slowly, with her floaters nearby. Having learnt to swim recently, she was surprisingly confident in the water and soon began having a lot of fun with me around. I, on the other hand, kept worrying and stayed within arm's reach of her. Once my wife joined us, I managed to squeeze in a few short laps of swimming myself. Before we knew it, it was 6 PM, and we got out of the pool.

We freshened up, showered and got ready to head out for dinner. We settled on a restaurant called Oh Pondi in White Town. When we arrived, there was just one group of diners there, which immediately made us question our choice. But we decided to stay. Although Manchow soup wasn't on the menu, the staff were kind enough to prepare some for Ahilya. We ordered chicken Chettinad curry, another chicken starter and a prawn dish. Everything turned out to be tasty, but a lot spicier than I can normally handle. After dinner, all three of us were craving dessert, but Oh Pondi had limited options. So we decided to look elsewhere and were lucky to find what turned out to be the best brownie with ice cream just a few metres down the street.

By this time, Ahilya was really sleepy and insisted on going back to the hotel. She actually fell asleep on the scooter on the way back. We reached our room, spoke briefly about the next day's plan and went to bed.

<h2 id="day-3">Day 3: Paradise Beach and a Change of Hotels</h2>

Our plan for the day was to wake up early, have breakfast and reach the Chunnambar Boat House before 9:30 AM. That would hopefully get us to Paradise Beach by around 10 AM, allowing us to avoid the hottest part of the day. Alas, we woke up at 8 AM, and by the time we finished breakfast, it was already 10 AM. We also had to check out of our hotel that day. I settled the bill and asked the staff to hold on to our luggage for a few hours.

The Chunnambar Boat House is where you board a ferry to reach Paradise Beach. The boat ride itself takes roughly 20 minutes. We bought our tickets, and luckily, there wasn't a huge queue, unlike what we'd read about online.

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day3/Chunambar-Boat-House.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Chunnambar Boat House">

A ferry was already waiting to leave, so we got in, made ourselves comfortable and clicked a few pictures. Ahilya got a little cranky and insisted on sitting at the edge of the boat, which made me uncomfortable. Thankfully, one of us was always holding her hand.

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day3/Chunambar-Boat-House-2.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="On the ferry to Paradise Beach">

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day3/Enroute-Paradise-1.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="En route to Paradise Beach through the backwaters">

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day3/Enroute-Paradise-2.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="The Chunnambar backwaters">

After the boat docked, we had a short walk of around ten minutes to reach the beach. We finally got there at around 11:40 AM, and we immediately realised what our late start had cost us.

It was around 31°C, but the temperature felt closer to 39°C, according to Google. The heat was unbearable. The sand was so hot that walking barefoot on it was almost impossible. There were a few makeshift shelters where people could take refuge from the sun.

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day3/Paradise-beach.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Paradise Beach">

We were in two minds about whether we should enter the water, but Ahilya was too excited, and there were already quite a few people playing in the sea. We walked closer to the shore, kept our footwear and other belongings well away from the waterline, and stepped into the sea. The water felt pleasant. Within no time, Ahilya began having a blast, splashing around and playing in the water. The heat that had felt unbearable just a few minutes earlier suddenly seemed a lot more manageable.

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day3/Paradise-beach-1.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Playing in the water at Paradise Beach">

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day3/Paradise-Beach-2.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Paradise Beach shoreline">

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day3/Paradise-beach-3.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Afternoon at Paradise Beach">

After playing for around 30–40 minutes and clicking goofy pictures, we decided to leave. I was genuinely disappointed by the lack of decent changing rooms and toilets nearby. The facilities that were available were dirty and inconveniently located. In general, I've found Goa's beaches to be more tourist-friendly when it comes to basic facilities. I couldn't say the same about Paradise Beach. We grabbed some lime juice from the only makeshift eatery we could find and headed back towards the boat. We didn't have to wait long.

Our ferry arrived after just five minutes, and we were back at the Chunnambar Boat House by around 1:50 PM. I'd spotted an ice cream shop within the boat house premises and decided to surprise Ahilya with ice cream without waiting for her to ask for it. She was elated! We got three scoops: guava, Biscoff and blueberry. Instead of a regular waffle cone, they served the ice cream in a waffle cup, which was a nice little twist. After a couple of stops for raw mango and coconut water, we headed back to the hotel. Once there, I returned the rented scooter and settled the remaining amount. We collected our luggage and waited for our Ola to take us to our next hotel, White Pearl Residency, which was around 9 km away and closer to Auroville.

After checking in, we initially thought we'd grab some evening snacks since we'd skipped lunch. But we eventually decided against it and instead headed out to explore Auroville Beach, which was nearby. We freshened up, got ready and walked to the beach. Unlike Paradise Beach, Auroville Beach was crowded, and there was quite a bit of plastic, wrappers and other garbage lying around. It was a real put-off. Since we were a little hungry, we grabbed some pineapple and coconut water and found a spot to sit and relax. We began making sandcastles and all sorts of other structures in the sand.

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day3/Playing-with-sand1.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Sandcastles at Auroville Beach">

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day3/Playing-with-sand-2.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Playing in the sand">

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/day3/Auroville-sand-play.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Evening at Auroville Beach">

Unlike the scorching heat at Paradise Beach that morning, the weather here was pleasant. The sun was about to set, and a cool breeze felt soothing against the skin. It was genuinely relaxing. After a while, a policeman arrived and began asking people to stay out of the water. We decided it was time to leave.

By the time we reached our room, we were absolutely starving. We decided to order dinner, and as luck would have it, the hotel's in-house restaurant was running a buy-one-get-one-free pizza offer. We ordered a Margherita pizza along with some ghee rice and dal. The wait wasn't long. The moment the pizza arrived, all three of us pounced on it like animals who hadn't eaten in days! The classic Margherita was great: thin crust, just the right amount of cheese, and absolutely delicious. The ghee rice and dal were the icing on the cake. We'd been longing for some familiar comfort food over the previous three days, and finding it here, tasting just the way we'd hoped, was incredibly soothing. I think it did more than just satisfy our hunger. It nourished our souls.

We went to bed soon after.

<h2 id="day-4">Day 4: Pondicherry to Mahabalipuram to Chennai</h2>

Our return flight was at 11 AM on October 4 from Chennai. Travelling from Pondicherry to Chennai Airport on the morning of our flight would have meant waking up very early and making a three-hour journey, with the added uncertainty of traffic. We decided it would be better to reach Chennai a day earlier.

The plan for Day 4 was to check out of our hotel, visit Mahabalipuram on the way to Chennai, spend a few hours exploring its historically significant monuments and then continue to Chennai for the night. We had booked a cab from Pondicherry. Our driver was in his sixties and boasted about having once driven R. Venkataraman, a former President of India. In fact, he seemed to enjoy boasting in general! After initially trying to strike up a conversation, I realised it was probably better to keep quiet and let him drive.

We checked out of our hotel at around 11 AM and set off towards Chennai. Since we wanted to visit Mahabalipuram along the way, we took the East Coast Road, which runs along the Bay of Bengal. The ECR was indeed beautiful. Although the map makes it look as though the road runs close to the coast, you don't always get to see the ocean because stretches of vegetation, settlements and other development lie between the road and the shoreline.

We reached Mahabalipuram at around 1:15 PM. Our first stop was Krishna's Butterball, a massive natural rock stading on a sloping rock surface. It is astonishing to see how this enormous rock appears to balance on the incline without rolling down. It looks as if a small nudge and the rock would go tumbling away, yet it has remained there for ages.

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/Day4/Krishna's ButterBall.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Krishna's Butterball, Mahabalipuram">

The surrounding area also has several historic rock-cut structures, a triple celled monument that has the trinity deities and sculptures dating back to the Pallava period - Ganesha Ratha, Varaha cave, Raya Gopuram (a temple gateway) (from the Vijayanagara empire)

We had decided to skip a proper lunch and make do with snacks along the way. We bought some raw mango and munched on it while exploring the site. We ended up spending more time here than expected. Our driver grew impatient and called us to hurry up, but we took our own sweet time.

From there, we walked towards another famous monument, Arjuna's Penance. Arjuna's Penance is a massive open-air natural rock face with carvings on it. It features a remarkable collection of figures, including deities, humans and animals, with elephants being among the most striking. The scene is commonly associated with the descent of the River Ganga from heaven to earth, although it is also interpreted as depicting Arjuna's penance to obtain a divine weapon from Lord Shiva. The sheer amount of detail carved into the rock is incredible. You can stand there looking at it for a while and still keep discovering something new.

We then headed back to where our vehicle was parked, and our driver took us to the Shore Temple. The Shore Temple is one of Mahabalipuram's most iconic monuments. Unlike the rock-cut structures we'd seen earlier, this is a structural temple built using blocks of stone. It dates to the early eighth century and stands right by the Bay of Bengal. It is not regular functioning temple. Today, it is primarily a protected heritage monument, although it was originally built as a place of worship.

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/Day4/Shore-temple-1.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="The Shore Temple, Mahabalipuram">

Fences separate the monument complex from the beach, but its location is spectacular. The sight of an ancient stone temple standing so close to the sea is something else. The stonework and the carvings reminded me of the rock-cut monuments at Ajanta and Ellora. We spent some time there and clicked a few pictures. Ahilya wanted to hang from the low-hanging branches of a tree, so we let her do that and clicked a few more pictures before heading back towards the parking area.

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/Day4/shore-temple2.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="The Shore Temple by the Bay of Bengal">

Along the way, we also picked up some gifts for family back home.

We thought we were done for the day, but there was one final site left to visit. Our driver took us to the Pancha Rathas. The Pancha Rathas, or the Five Rathas, are a group of massive monolithic structures carved out of individual rock formations. Each structure is carved from a single large piece of stone with impresssive craftsmanship. The five structures are named after the Pandavas and Draupadi from the Mahabharata: Draupadi Ratha, Arjuna Ratha, Bhima Ratha, Dharmaraja Ratha, and the Nakula-Sahadeva Ratha. Bhima Ratha is largest of them, while Dharmaraja Ratha is the tallest of the group.

<img src="/assets/images/pondicherry/Day4/Nakula-sahadeva-ratha.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;" title="Nakula-Sahadeva Ratha">

What I found particularly interesting was that these structures were never completed or consecrated as functioning temples. Their names are traditional associations with the characters of the Mahabharata, rather than proof that each structure was actually built for a particular Pandava. That got me thinking: how did people figure out that these structures were associated with the five Pandavas in the first place? The information boards explained that the original purpose of the structures remains uncertain. They are believed to have been left unfinished, possibly following the death of the Pallava king Narasimhavarman I. Something so elaborate, carved with such precision, and yet we dont know exactly what its creators intended it to be.

All three of the main monument groups we visited, including the Shore Temple, Arjuna's Penance and the Pancha Rathas, form part of the UNESCO World Heritage-listed Group of Monuments at Mahabalipuram. We had already spent close to three hours exploring Mahabalipuram, and we wanted to reach Chennai before it got too late. We left at around 5 PM and reached Chennai at close to 7 PM. The distance was only around 50–60 km, depending on the route, but it still took us nearly two hours. Our hotel for the night was tucked away in the interiors of Pammal, although it was relatively close to the airport.

By then, we had no energy left to venture out for dinner. Thankfully, we ordered food to our room, ate and watched a little television before calling it a night.

<h2 id="day-5">Day 5: Chennai to Pune</h2>

We woke up early the next morning because we'd decided to reach the airport by 9 AM, giving ourselves enough time before our 11 AM flight. We'd been told that morning traffic could cause delays getting to the airport from our hotel, so we decided not to take any chances. We got ready and had breakfast. It wasn't as good as what we'd enjoyed over the previous few days, but it wasn't bad either. Luckily, the hotel provided a complimentary airport drop. We finished breakfast, checked out and hopped into the car. We were at the airport in just another 20 minutes.

We checked in our luggage, collected our boarding passes and settled down for a bit. Ahilya wanted popcorn, so we got her some. We also grabbed a coffee, and before we knew it, we were in the boarding queue. A couple of hours later, we were in a cab in Pune, heading back home.

And just like that, our Pondicherry trip came to an end.

## To sum it up

It was a great trip, with a little bit of annoyance thanks to the hot and humid weather, a little hassle because of the language barrier, plenty of good food, beautiful heritage villas, decent beaches and fascinating historical monuments.

But most importantly, we made some great memories for Ahilya together as a family. And I suppose that's what matters most.
