The simplest definition of individuality as I have known until now is "freedom to be oneself." And at surface level, it sounds unquestionably good.

Over time, individuality has been romanticised into phrases like:

*"I should be accepted exactly as I am."*

*"I need someone who accepts me for who I am."*

This romanticised version of individuality sounds great for the set of people that lack a very important ability - the ability to reflect upon one's actions and how those actions impact themselves and others that are close to them.

The more I thought about this and philosophised about it with my philosophy partner (ChatGPT), the more I'm convinced that this romanticised view of individuality is one of the reasons people are so easily offended today.

## Individuality has limits

Anyone who truly understands that no human being lives alone - and that one has to co-exist with other people's individualities - understands that any form of association with another human being limits individuality to some extent. Freedom to be oneself can absolutely exist alongside responsibility and accountability. It must.

Individuality is not an unlimited licence to behave however one wishes. Whenever we become a part of society, workplace, family, or relationship, living alongside other humans means an agreement to voluntarily give up certain freedoms.

You can't show up at a funeral dressed inappropriately and claim that any objection is an attack on your individuality. If a workplace dictates that office hours start at 10:30, you can't invoke individuality on the grounds that you are not an early riser. That would be absurd.

What is the point of your individuality when it does not take into consideration circumstance, situation, and your responsibility towards others?

## Individuality with responsibility

Individuality without responsibility or accountability seems very problematic. Only when you think of individuality and responsibility together do you ask the genuinely reflecting questions:

*How does who I am affect the people around me?*

*What parts of my individuality are proving to be an inconvenience to others?*

When individuality becomes a reason to avoid reflecting upon the damage caused by one's behaviour, it becomes a shield against accountability. And any meaningful association between human beings eventually breaks down when accountability disappears.

## Aristotle's view

I briefly read a few paragraphs about Aristotle's view on individuality and find myself conforming to it more than to the romanticised version.

His view says that individuality is deeply social. It says:

*"I must cultivate virtues that make me flourish as well as allow others around me to flourish."*

One cannot truly flourish in total isolation in order to be happy. This view resonates with me more, as it clearly emphasises that individual excellence doesn't have to mean only looking after yourself. It means collective flourishment.

## A final thought

Individuality does not give anyone immunity from consequences. Maybe you are disorganised, blunt, ill-tempered, introverted. Maybe you like certain things and not others - and these could be your genuine personality traits. But as reasoning adults, how we balance our individuality and take ownership of our responsibilities, and how we account for the way our traits affect those around us - that is what defines our character.

A mature take on individuality is not:

*"People should accept me as I am."*

It is:

*"I remain myself while willingly developing virtues that allow me to live well with others."*

It is not demanding unconditional acceptance. It is about preserving who you are while trying to become a person with whom others can also flourish.
