title: "A short ride chasing waterfalls; high on school time nostalgia"

Few weeks ago, I came across a recommendation on Reddit about a riding circuit from Pune towards Khandi, a village in the Maval region, leading to Bendewadi Waterfalls. It had been on my list ever since. It had already been a few weeks since I had hit the roads purely for a long ride, and I had been itching to get back on them. But something or the other every weekend kept me away from it. Even last night, while I was still contemplating the ride, I almost gave up on the plan because of a bad body ache.

Anyway, I woke up in the morning excited, and thankfully, the body ache was gone. Got ready, plugged in my headphones, set the route on Maps and off I was. But I was missing something. Yes. My morning kicker, tea. I took my first stop at Yewale Amrutulya, fuelled myself up with a piping hot cup of tea - food for my soul - and set off again, only to realise that I was already short on fuel. I spotted a petrol pump a few minutes later and fuelled up. I had been ignoring the uneasiness my headphones were causing because I had pulled my skullcap over my ears, and the helmet was pressing them further in. I adjusted the headphones and the skullcap a bit, found the sweet spot and off I was again.

As soon as I hit the highway, it started drizzling and, almost as if the playlist knew what was happening, it chose to play *Mastana Mausam Hai, Rangeen Nazara*. What serendipity. Amazing high I got.

A few kilometres ahead of Vadgaon, on the Old Pune-Mumbai Highway, you have to break right towards the Jambhul/Takwe side. The maps showed that my destination was still a good 30 km away from here. This road takes you towards Takwe, which has quite a few automotive manufacturing plants - Mahindra Accelo, Mahindra Spare Parts Manufacturing, Gestamp etc. In spite of the industrial presence, the roads on this route are quite bumpy, with lots of potholes and stones. The road condition doesn't change much until a village named "Mau", after which things keep alternating between cement roads for a few kilometres, followed by stretches of potholes, unmade roads and roads under construction.

It is mostly after Mau that the breathtaking views start. I hadn't eaten anything since morning and it was 9:30, the time by which I'm usually done with my breakfast. I was hungry and decided to take a pit stop for breakfast. Just behind the small hotel I stopped at, there was a small waterfall. The hotel owner told me that there are numerous such waterfalls on the way. After scribbling a bit in my notebook, my breakfast arrived. I finished it hurriedly and set off further.

There indeed are quite a few waterfalls adorning the hills on this route. Khandi is known for this. During the monsoon, the road around the Thokarwadi dam backwaters (the dam is built on river Andra - a tributary of Indrayani) is dotted with numerous large and small waterfalls. There is a picnic spot named Jagtap Waterfall Agro Tourism where they seemed to have built a few experiences for kids and families around waterfalls - made a mental note to bring Kittu here; she'll love it.

<img src="/assets/images/ride-chasing-waterfalls/2.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;">

Just a few kilometres ahead was probably the most scenic patch of the ride. Picture this - on your left, you have lush green mountains with occasional waterfalls running down them, adorning the mountains like a white necklace, and on your right you have the calm, serene waterbody (backwaters of Thokarwadi). And you are slowly cruising on a cement road, humming *Yun Hi Chala Chal Rahi* - so blissful. Throughout this patch, I kept stopping to take pictures. My photography skills aren't great, but I'm sure a location like this is a goldmine for nature photographers.

A short, bumpy ride from there and I spotted a small shack selling tea. I bought myself a cup, but because there was no network, I couldn't pay him. The seller very graciously said that it was okay. He asked me to take a picture of his scanner and pay him later. Which I did as soon as I got network. Kind man.

The village where this waterfall is located is called Dahuli. Just a kilometre away, you'll find a board that directs you towards the waterfall. Only, it doesn't quite take you directly to the waterfall. It takes you to a parking area controlled by a hotel that also manages access to the waterfall entrance. I paid Rs. 20 for entry. From there, it is a short walk across a plateau alongside paddy fields to reach the waterfall.

<img src="/assets/images/ride-chasing-waterfalls/5.jpg" style="width: 100%;border:solid 1px #ccc; border-radius:2px;">

The waterfall is cute. There were already a few folks enjoying their time in it. The water was clean and crystal clear. I hadn't brought spare clothes, so I didn't dare go under the waterfall. I clicked some pictures and then noticed something interesting. The hill that held this waterfall had a cave where a few people had set up tables selling corn, tea and Maggi. I was amused. You cannot really spot this cave unless you're standing at a height facing the waterfall. I made my way there and bought some corn. I sat inside the cave, savouring the corn and looking at the waterfall from behind. It reminded me of a scene from Batman where the Batcave is hidden behind a huge waterfall. This waterfall wasn't huge or wide though.

I spent some time there, clicked a few pictures and then started my return journey. On my way back, I was particularly stunned at one spot. Just ahead of a turn, there was this wide hill standing tall, a waterfall on its right, and part of the hill shone brighter than the rest because of the sunlight. I stopped there for a while. Taking it all in. Admiring the canvas nature had painted. Because the waterfall was far away, the optical illusion made it look like it was moving in slow motion. It felt as if time had slowed down. Breathtaking moment.

The fatigue had started to set in, so I kept taking small breaks after every 10 to 15 minutes of riding. Once I got back on the highway, a short traffic jam irritated me for a bit, but the songs in my playlist helped me get my calm back. This bike ride was a bit high on school- and college-time nostalgia because of the playlist I was listening to. Beautiful songs from the early 2000s running up to 2010 kept me company throughout the ride. My favourite songs from *Kyun! Ho Gaya Na*, *Armaan*, from *Khakee*, *Swades*, *Run* and others kept playing in the background, each one of them giving a little reminder of my school and college days.

I was back home by 2:00 pm. Chasing waterfalls, discovering picturesque spots, reliving school-time nostalgia and listening to some amazing songs, all packed into just over 120 km and a little over six hours. All in all, a great ride.
